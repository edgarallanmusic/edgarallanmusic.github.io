The fact that one becomes what one is
Presupposes that one has not the remotest suspicion of what one is

In the mirror, broken days,
I watched my shadow thin;
a twisted soul in borrowed flame,
the poison folded in.
Every scar became a map,
every fall a place to stand;
I let the saint inside me die —
a satyr took my hand.

Nobody told me where I'm going,
nothing in me knew the way.

I become what I am,
without a map or aim;
a spark that never asked
the meaning of the flame.

Thirty-six, a lamp turned low, I couldn't read three steps away,
the age my father faded at — and I was fading the same way;
in my father I'm already gone, in my mother I grow old,
a decadent and a beginning, I'm the ember and the cold.
Shadow summer, sunless winter, every curtain drawn for good,
but the sickness taught me tasting, little things the way I should:
bread and weather, wind and window, light that crawls across the floor —
I got well because I wanted, and I wanted nothing more.

Nobody told me who I'd be;
the fever wrote it down for me.

I become what I am,
half the dusk and half the dawn;
a spark that never asked
which side it's burning on.

I traded thorns for vines,
let the ivy find my brow,
and I screamed yes — not to a fight —
to all of it, then and now.

Not a hero. Never was.
Just a fever that learned to sing.

I become what I am,
without a map or aim;
a spark that never asked
the meaning of the flame.

...and I never knew what I am.
