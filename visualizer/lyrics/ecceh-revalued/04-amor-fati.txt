Autumn noon, the grapes are browning,
not a ripple on the lake;
I looked behind me, then ahead,
and nothing asked to be remade.
Forty-four, I laid the year down —
it had the right to go;
whatever in it was alive
is saved, and won't grow old.

Let it all come round again,
the fever and the wine;
I wouldn't change a single hour —
amor fati, it was mine.

By the lake at Silvaplana
a pyramid of stone;
six thousand feet past man and time,
the thought struck me alone.
Not a sigh disturbs the open sea,
I'd never wish it new;
the future spreads like broad, calm water —
I wouldn't change its blue.

Let it all come round again,
the sickness and the shore;
I wouldn't move a single stone —
amor fati, nothing more.

Not only bear what has to be —
love it, all of it, and stay.

Let it all come round again,
the darkness and the bloom;
how could I not be thankful
to every hour, even the tomb.
